SCENE 1  MARLEY'S GHOST

Scrooge is in his nightclothes. The door with a door knocker is brought on by Jacob Marley (unseen), with him standing behind it ready to poke his head through the opening behind the knocker.

SCROOGE    (Reaching the door) Home at last.

JACOB MARLEY    (Poking his head through the door) Ebenezer Scrooge!

SCROOGE    (Terrified) Aargh! What are you? (Not believing his eyes) Jacob Marley?

The head vanishes and the chains exit.

SCROOGE    (Rubbing his eyes) Pull yourself together, Scrooge. Marley's dead! Dead as a door knocker! (Realizing what he's said) Oh, I think I need to go to Specsavers!

Scrooge goes indoors and the door backs offstage. Scrooge takes off his coat, hat and scarf and puts on the dressing gown, nightcap and slippers. He mimes locking the door and sits in one of the chairs reading a newspaper. He eats bread and cheese and occasionally looks around him apprehensively. Suddenly there is the sound of chains clanking. Scrooge looks up, alarmed.

CLANKING CHAINS SFX

NARRATOR 5    As he sat in the chilly gloom, an eerie jangling sound assaulted Scrooge's ears.
  In any ordinary man, considering the icy temperature in the room, the sound might be put down to the noise of his own knees knocking, or the chattering of his teeth. Scrooge, however, was acclimatized to the cold world in which he chose to live. The clanking grew louder and seemed to draw ever closer, until in one fearful, ghostly moment that froze Scrooge to the spot, a dismal vision entered through the locked door, like mist through gauze.

Jacob Marley enters, looking grey, ghostly and bound in plastic chains. He is surrounded by a group of ghosts wearing white sheets or white outfits with trailing pieces of fabric. They 'whoo' in an exaggerated way.

SONG 3.  HE WAS JACOB MARLEY

SCROOGE    (Terrified, he ducks down behind the bed) Marley? Jacob Marley? Is that really you? No, I won't believe it! I just had too much cheese at dinner.

SCENE 10  IN THE CITY

The table is moved to one side of the stage and covered in old blankets and ragged clothes. A sign is put on top or hung nearby: 'JOE'S PAWN SHOP'.

BUSINESSMAN 1    How are you, old fellow?

BUSINESSMAN 2    Shocking news about the share price, eh what?

BUSINESSMAN 3    Indeed! I'll be glad to get away for the season.

NARRATOR 5    Scrooge found himself in the heart of the city, a place where he often did business. Standing in a group were several gentlemen whose faces he knew. They were the sort of men whose waistbands stretched much further than their compassion or imagination.

The two Ragged Children enter, shivering, and huddle near the businessmen.

SCENE 11  THE CRATCHIT HOUSE IN MOURNING

The stage is set up for the Cratchits' house. The Cratchits enter (minus Tiny Tim) and place Tiny Tim's crutch by his chair. Scrooge and The Spirit of Christmas Yet To Come (SPIRIT FUTURE) unfreeze and turn back towards the audience.

NARRATOR 5    As Scrooge fled from the hateful scene, it seemed as though the Cratchit's house was but a whisper away. But the solemn silence which now enveloped this humble dwelling screamed in Scrooge's soul, causing him to stare in fearful realization at the gathered company within, which did not include young Tim.

The Cratchit family sit or stand looking at Tiny Tim's crutch, which lies unused by his chair. Some neighbours call by, in a processional way, bringing flowers and cards.

SCENE 12  CHRISTMAS MORNING AT THE CRATCHITS'

NARRATOR 4    He smiled as he noticed the spirit's dust still glittering in the doorway. He vowed that from now on he would do his best to spread the spirit of Christmas throughout the whole year.

Scrooge mimes knocking on the door of the Cratchit's house, which is positioned offstage.

SCROOGE    (In a stern voice) Bob Cratchit!

The Cratchit family come out.

BOB CRATCHIT    Mr Scrooge! I thought you had agreed that we could have today off.

SCROOGE    That's just not good enough, Bob Cratchit!

MRS CRATCHIT    It's only once a year, Mr Scrooge!

SCROOGE    No! Not good enough at all. You must have tomorrow off as well and then we must arrange for you to have an increase in salary, Bob!

BOB CRATCHIT    Mr Scrooge, I don't know what to say.

SCROOGE    Just say that you'll forgive me, Bob, and that from now on you'll let me be not just your employer, but your friend.

They shake hands.

MRS CRATCHIT    It seems that Christmas really is a time for miracles!

NARRATOR 5    Scrooge was better than his word. He changed so much that it was rumoured that some folk called him 'Have-a-knees-up' Scrooge. He was so grateful that Tiny Tim did not die. He became like a second father to him, sparing no expense and ensuring that he always had the best of care.

NARRATOR 6    He kept the spirit of Christmas alive throughout the year, taking care to show kindness and compassion towards all his fellow men and to begin and end each day with a thankful heart for the love which had so transformed his life - a love which he realized was so clearly demonstrated at that very first Christmas long ago. And so, as Tiny Tim would say, 'God bless us every one!'
